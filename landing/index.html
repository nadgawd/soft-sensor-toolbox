The problem

The variable that matters most is often the one you measure least.

Lab assays take hours. Online analysers are expensive, drift, and need constant maintenance. In between, operators run the plant on temperatures and pressures — and hope the quality follows.

01

Lab results arrive late

A four-hour assay means four hours of production you cannot correct. Off-spec material is found after it is made.

02

Analysers cost to own

Sampling systems, shelters, calibration and spares make a single online analyser a capital project of its own.

03

The data is already there

Your historian holds years of process and lab data. A soft sensor learns the relationship — and estimates quality continuously.

Workflow

From a spreadsheet of plant data to a live estimate, in four steps.

  1. 1

    Upload & profile

    Drop in a CSV or Excel export from your historian. The toolbox profiles every column, detects the time axis, and flags gaps, constants and outliers.

  2. 2

    Clean & choose variables

    Handle missing values and bad ranges, pick the lab variable to predict, and select the process variables that explain it — with guidance at every step.

  3. 3

    Train & validate

    Fit PLS, PCR, Ridge, Lasso, OLS or k-NN models on a split you draw yourself. Compare them on parity, residual and importance plots — and read the final model as a plain equation.

  4. 4

    Deploy & monitor

    Publish the model as a live endpoint. Plant connectors stream readings in; the monitor tracks estimates against new lab results and warns on drift.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

A soft sensor is a model that estimates a hard-to-measure quality variable from easy-to-measure process variables — temperatures, pressures, flows — trained on historical lab results. It runs continuously, filling the gap between lab assays.

A historian export with a timestamp, the process variables, and the lab variable you want to predict, aligned by sample time. A few hundred matched lab samples is a practical starting point.

PLS, PCR, Ridge, Lasso, ordinary least squares and k-NN — with train/validation/test splits you control, cross-validated diagnostics, and the final model exported as a plain equation.

Through lightweight connectors for OPC UA, MQTT, PI Web API and CSV files. They send readings to an authenticated endpoint, buffer locally during outages, and need only outbound HTTPS.
